5. Barrier option: closed form vs PDE vs discretely-monitored MC
==============================================================================
vanilla call (no barrier)                               10.45058357
down-and-out call, closed form (continuous)              8.66547166

Discrete monitoring bias in Monte Carlo, validated against the
Broadie-Glasserman-Kou continuity correction, which predicts that a
discretely-monitored barrier behaves like a continuous one at a barrier
shifted away from the spot by exp(-0.5826 * vol * sqrt(T/m)):

  (a discretely-monitored knock-out is always worth MORE: fewer observation
   dates mean fewer chances to breach. Matching BGK at every frequency shows
   the gap is the predicted discretisation bias, not an implementation error)

==============================================================================
quotes inverted   : 28 / 28
needed fallback   : 7  (Newton stalled on near-zero vega)
usable for a fit  : 25  (rest are ill-conditioned, see below)

max error over WELL-CONDITIONED points : 1.130e-06 vol points
max error over ALL converged points    : 2.345e-01 vol points

  The second number looks alarming and is entirely expected: in the deep
  wings at short expiry, vega is ~1e-11, so the quote pins down no vol at
  all. The solver still returns a root that reprices correctly -- it simply
  flags that the root carries no information. A real chain is full of these,
  and feeding them to a smile fit would corrupt it.
